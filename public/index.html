PulseVault

Your records. Your consent. Your key.

An ABHA-linked, QR-based, consent-driven health vault. Patients carry their own record. Any provider (PHC, clinic or district hospital) sees allergies, medicines and tests in seconds, with no login, no hardware and no training. Consent is scoped, time-bound and revocable in one tap.

The problem

Records don't follow the patient

The care gap

A patient's history is split across PHCs, private clinics and district hospitals. Each record stays with the provider that created it, so the next doctor sees none of it.

Real-world bottlenecks

  • Repeated tests: earlier diagnostics unavailable, so patients pay twice.
  • Unsafe prescribing: unknown allergies and current medicines risk adverse drug events.
  • Clinic friction: small providers lack the staff, hardware or time for complex EHRs.

Who benefits

  • Patients (rural, elderly, chronic-care): history travels with them, and they control who sees it.
  • PHC doctors & small clinics: critical history in seconds.
  • District hospitals: fewer duplicate investigations.
  • Health system: less avoidable spend and harm.

Gaps in existing frameworks

  • ABHA/ABDM supplies identity and rails, but PHC adoption is limited by login-heavy workflows.
  • Consent is often a static checkbox, not scoped, time-bound and revocable.
  • Most tools assume stable internet and registered facilities.

The four requirements PulseVault is built around

1 Patient-held record

via QR, ABHA linkage, or both (hybrid).

2 Granular consent + instant revocation

a live engine, not a static checkbox.

3 Access in seconds

no complex login, heavy hardware or training.

4 Instant critical history

prescriptions, tests and allergies at a glance.

Idea & solution

Consent that's live, not a checkbox

Consent & Revocation Engine

  • Generate: pick scope (allergies, medicines, conditions, tests), duration (15 min · 1 h · 24 h), view-only.
  • Issue: signed single-purpose QR, or a 6-digit code fallback.
  • Monitor: live “provider viewing, 08:42 left” plus an audit trail.
  • Revoke: one tap. The token dies and access ends on the next request.

Doctor point-of-care flow

  • Scan: phone camera or the light web scanner. No install, no login.
  • Critical Snapshot: allergies, current medicines, chronic conditions, last tests.
  • Expand within the granted scope only.
  • Propose notes or prescriptions; the patient approves. A rule-based allergy cross-check warns on conflicts.

Emergency offline QR

  • Signed blood group, allergies and chronic medicines.
  • Readable without internet.
  • Patient opt-in, minimal by design.
  • Hybrid identity: ABHA optional. Start with a phone number, link ABHA later.

Innovation & USP

Live consent

Scope and expiry are baked into every signed token and checked on every request, so revocation is instant.

Zero-login providers

Any phone or PC with a camera. Removes the adoption barrier for small clinics.

Hybrid identity

ABHA-linked or phone/QR-only, so nobody is excluded.

Security, not buzzwords

Value comes from E2EE, signed tokens and audit logs, not from “using AI”. No ML model is needed for this problem, so none is used.

Data flow: consent → access → revoke

  1. Patient picks scope + duration. The scoped snapshot is encrypted on the device; the ciphertext goes to the token service, the key stays in the QR's URL fragment (#k=…), which browsers never send to servers.
  2. Provider scans. The service verifies the HMAC-signed token, expiry and revoke flag on every request and returns ciphertext only.
  3. The provider's browser decrypts locally and renders a view-only Critical Snapshot.
  4. Patient taps Revoke. The ciphertext is destroyed and the provider's next poll (≤2 s) is refused; the screen clears.
  5. Every event lands in a hash-chained audit log.

Tech stack

  • Frontend: installable PWA (vanilla JS, no framework), offline shell via service worker.
  • Backend: Netlify Functions (serverless) + Netlify Blobs.
  • Standards: FHIR R4 (Patient, AllergyIntolerance, MedicationRequest, Condition, DiagnosticReport), ABDM-style consent artefacts.
  • Security: AES-256-GCM E2EE, ECDSA-signed emergency QR, HMAC-SHA256 tokens, strict CSP, rate-limited code fallback.
  • Roadmap: ZKP selective disclosure.
Feasibility & roadmap

Built for small facilities

Why it works at a PHC

  • No new hardware: any smartphone, tablet or PC browser with a camera.
  • No training: scan → see.
  • Low bandwidth: a tiny PWA with an encrypted payload of a few KB.
  • Offline fallback: the emergency QR needs no connection.
  • Inclusive: ABHA optional; link later.
  • Scalable: stateless serverless services; aligned to ABDM interoperability and DPDP Act principles. Production ABDM needs sandbox registration and certification.
